Frequently asked questions

How projects work with PS Web Studio, from first message to launch and support.

Get a quote

What kind of websites does PS Web Studio build?

Business websites, travel and tourism sites, healthcare and clinic sites, and service-business sites. Work is frontend-led: responsive layouts, fast pages, clean markup and on-page SEO, with MySQL and AJAX integration where a site needs forms, listings or an admin-managed content area.

Who will work on my project?

PS Web Studio is run by a working frontend developer based in Mumbai. You deal with the person building your site, not an account manager passing notes. If a project needs a specialist (a copywriter or a photographer, for example), that is discussed and agreed with you first.

Do you have experience in my industry?

The portfolio covers travel, healthcare and service businesses. Travel and tourism is the deepest area: itinerary pages, destination content, enquiry flows and SEO structure for tour sites. See the Projects page for real examples before you decide.

Can you redesign or fix my existing website?

Yes. Redesigns, speed fixes, mobile layout problems, broken forms and SEO clean-ups are all common requests. Send the URL with your quote request. A short review of the current site comes first, so the scope reflects what is actually wrong rather than a full rebuild by default.

How do I start a project?

Use the Get a Quote form, or message on WhatsApp. The more you include, the faster the reply:

  • What the business does and who the site is for
  • Pages or features you expect
  • Two or three websites you like, and why
  • Your target launch date and rough budget

You get a scope and price within one business day.

What are the steps from brief to launch?
  • Scope: pages, features and content agreed in writing.
  • Design: layout and visual direction shared for your feedback.
  • Build: responsive development and any database or form integration.
  • Review: you test on a staging link and send one consolidated list of changes.
  • Launch: domain, hosting, SEO basics and final checks.
How long does a website take?

It depends on page count and features, and above all on how quickly content and feedback arrive. A small brochure site is typically a matter of weeks. A larger site with listings, enquiry flows or a database takes longer. The exact timeline goes into your quote, and you can pick a preferred window in the quote form.

Content delays are the most common cause of late launches
Do I need to provide the text and images?

Ideally yes, since you know the business best. If you have rough notes, they can be shaped into page copy. Images should be real photos of your business, team or destinations where possible. Stock photos work as placeholders but rarely build trust. Tell us up front what you can supply so it is reflected in the timeline.

How many rounds of changes are included?

The number of revision rounds is stated in your quote. Changes within the agreed scope are covered. New pages or features added mid-project are quoted separately, so the original price stays clear. Sending feedback as one consolidated list per round keeps things moving.

How much does a website cost?

There is no fixed price list because scope varies too much. Cost is driven by:

  • Number of pages and templates
  • Custom design versus a lighter layout
  • Features: enquiry forms, listings, database, admin panel
  • Content and SEO work included

Send the brief and you receive a written breakdown, so you can see what each part costs.

How does payment work?

Payment is split into milestones, with an advance to begin and the balance tied to delivery. The schedule and accepted payment methods are written into your quote before any work starts. For larger projects, more milestones can be agreed.

What costs are not included in the build price?

Third-party costs are usually separate and paid to the provider directly, in your name:

  • Domain registration and yearly renewal
  • Hosting
  • Paid plugins, premium fonts or licensed images
  • Email, SMS or payment-gateway fees

These are listed in the quote so nothing appears later.

What technologies do you use?

HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript and jQuery for the frontend, with Bootstrap or Tailwind CSS for layout. MySQL and AJAX handle dynamic content such as listings, enquiry storage and admin-managed pages. The choice follows the project: a simple site gets a simple stack.

The work is frontend-first. If a project needs heavy custom server-side logic beyond that, you are told at the quote stage rather than after work begins.

Will my site work on phones and tablets?

Yes. Every site is built mobile-first and checked across common screen sizes and browsers before launch. Most visitors to travel and local-service sites arrive on a phone, so the mobile layout gets the same attention as desktop.

Do you handle SEO?

On-page and technical SEO are part of the build: clean heading structure, title and meta tags, image alt text, schema markup, sitemap and robots setup, page speed and internal linking. That gives a site a sound base to rank from.

Nobody can promise a specific ranking or a date for page one. Rankings also depend on content quality, competition and links over time. Anyone who guarantees otherwise is selling you something they do not control.

How do you keep the site fast?

Compressed and correctly sized images, lazy loading below the fold, minimal render-blocking scripts and lean CSS. Pages are tested before launch, and speed problems found on an older site are listed in the review step.

Can the site collect enquiries and send them to my email?

Yes. Enquiry and contact forms can email submissions to you, store them in a database, or both, with spam protection and a clear success message for the visitor. WhatsApp click-to-chat buttons can be added alongside.

Who owns the website and the code after delivery?

You do. Once the final payment is made, the site files, content and design for your project are yours. Domain and hosting accounts should be registered in your name from the start. That way you are never locked in to anyone, including PS Web Studio.

What happens after the site goes live?

A short post-launch window covers bugs in what was delivered. After that, updates, new pages, content changes and ongoing SEO can be handled as small paid tasks or a monthly arrangement. Which one fits depends on how often your site changes.

Can I edit the content myself later?

If you plan to update content often, say so in the brief. Sections such as listings or announcements can be driven from a database with a simple admin area. A static brochure site is cheaper and faster, but changes go through a developer. Choosing between the two early avoids a rebuild later.

What is the best way to reach you during a project?

WhatsApp for quick questions, email at pswebstudio.dev@gmail.com for files and approvals, and a call when something is easier to explain out loud. Written approvals on email keep a clear record of what was agreed.
